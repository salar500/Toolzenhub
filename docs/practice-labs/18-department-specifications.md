# 18. Department implementation specifications

Each department section is what a development session needs: pages and URLs, files to create, reused assets ([16](16-existing-assets-reuse.md)), new engines, datasets, content volume, UI specifics, department-specific acceptance and sanity checks, dependencies, risks and open decisions. General rules live in [09](09-technical-architecture.md) (architecture), [10](10-content-quality-standard.md) (content) and [13](13-acceptance-criteria.md) (criteria). Lessons are in [06](06-curriculum-specifications.md).

**Effort** is in **deliveries**: one delivery is one commit and push with a report, sized to fit one focused session on the 4 GB laptop. These are [Opinion] estimates.

## 0. Common to every department (built in W0)

### Department page `/practice-labs/<dept>/`

| Region | Content |
|---|---|
| Breadcrumb | Home › Practice Labs › <Department> |
| Intro | H1 (department title); a 2-sentence promise; who it is for (3 audience bullets); unique text of 150 words or more |
| Route | "Recommended order": a numbered list of home paths, with status badges (Available, Building, Planned) |
| Path cards | title, one-line outcome, number of published lessons, level span (Beginner → Advanced), runtime note ("Runs in your browser", "Needs local setup for some steps") |
| Tools | the standalone tools of this department (links) |
| Also useful | cross-listed paths from other departments, labelled "from <home department>" |
| Sidebar | department rail: groups = paths, items = path pages (only published) |

### Files

- `assets/js/practice-labs/registry/departments.js`: the department record `{ id, slug, title, promise, audience[], intro[], order[], crossListed[], tools[] }`.
- Department pages are generated by `learn-html.js` → `departmentBody()`.

### Acceptance

- Only published paths are linked.
- Planned paths appear as text with a "Planned" badge.
- 150 or more unique words.
- 390 px: no overflow and 44 px targets.

### New engines and components

1. **E5 ShopLite** (`assets/js/practice-labs/shoplite/`)
   - **Screens:** home and product list (search, category filter, sort); product detail; cart (quantity, remove, coupon); checkout (address form, delivery slot, "Pay on delivery" only); order confirmation; order history; signup; login; profile (name, phone, password change).
   - **Data:** 24 original products (stationery, home and bags, the same world as the SQL shop data); 3 coupons with rules (percentage, minimum order, expiry); 2 seeded users.
   - **Release mechanism:** `?release=stable|r-bugs-1|r-a11y|r-api`. Each bug is a guarded branch in the code: `if (bug("RB1-07")) {...}`.
   - **The bug registry:** `shoplite/bugs.js`, with `{ id, release, feature, area, severity, title, steps[], expected, actual, matcher: { feature, fieldKeywords[], behaviourKeywords[] } }`.
   - **r-bugs-1 (10 bugs, verified reproducible):**
     1. the coupon applies below its minimum order;
     2. quantity accepts 0 and negatives;
     3. the search is case-sensitive;
     4. sort by price sorts as text ("1000" before "200");
     5. the password-change form accepts a mismatched confirmation;
     6. the cart total rounds incorrectly for 3 items at ₹33.33;
     7. the signup email accepts "a@b" (no domain dot);
     8. the order history shows another seeded user's order after logout and login;
     9. the delivery slot allows a past date;
     10. the "Remove" button removes the wrong row after a sort.
   - **r-a11y (8):**
     1. a missing label on the search;
     2. contrast below 3:1 on the price;
     3. no focus outline on the cart button;
     4. the checkout error is shown only by colour;
     5. an image without alt;
     6. a modal that traps focus;
     7. headings out of order;
     8. a non-button div used as a button.
   - **Isolation:** its own HTML pages (generated by a small Nunjucks template), its own CSS and an in-memory state module. **No storage.** A banner says "Practice app. Do not enter real details."

2. **E4 Structured Writing Lab** (`practice-labs/engines/rubric/`)
   - **Templates:**
     - *test case:* id, title, preconditions, steps[], data, expected;
     - *bug report:* title, environment, steps[], expected, actual, severity, priority, evidence note;
     - *requirement questions:* list;
     - *test plan:* scope, out of scope, approach, environments, entry and exit criteria, risks, schedule.
   - **Rules:**
     - required fields;
     - a vague-word list (works, properly, correctly, fine, etc.);
     - the expected result has an observable verb or value;
     - one action per step (no "and then" chains of more than 2 verbs);
     - concrete data present (digits or quoted values);
     - severity and priority are consistent with the scenario facts declared in the exercise;
     - coverage keywords (with synonyms) per exercise.
   - **Output:** met and missed items with advice; the model answer after an attempt; export to Markdown, CSV (test cases) and plain text.

3. **Bug Hunting Simulator UI**
   - **Desktop:** ShopLite in an iframe on the left, the report form on the right.
   - **Phone:** ShopLite full width, plus a "Report a bug" bottom sheet.
   - **Matcher:** reports are matched against the release's bugs (feature, then keyword overlap at or above a threshold); duplicates are detected.
   - **Score:** found n of N, with area hints after 3 misses.

4. **E6 API Workbench and mock API** (W3), [07](07-interactive-tools-catalogue.md) E6.
   - **Endpoints** (`/practice-labs/api-sandbox/v1`):
     - `GET /products?search&category&sort&page&limit`
     - `GET /products/{id}`
     - `POST /auth/login` → a mock JWT (decodable with the JWT engine)
     - `GET/POST/PATCH/DELETE /cart/items`
     - `POST /orders`, `GET /orders`, `GET /orders/{id}`
   - **Errors:** 400 (validation details), 401, 403, 404, 409, 422 and 429 (after 20 requests a minute, with Retry-After).
   - **r-api seeded bugs (8):**
     1. 200 instead of 201 on create;
     2. PATCH replaces the whole item;
     3. 500 on an unknown id instead of 404;
     4. a stack trace in an error body;
     5. orders of other users visible with any token (access control);
     6. pagination skips the last item;
     7. a negative quantity is accepted;
     8. a price returned as a string.

## D2 Test Automation Engineering (`/practice-labs/test-automation/`)

### Launch scope

W4: P07 Playwright. W8: P08 API Test Automation. W9: P09 Selenium with Java, P10 Framework and CI.

### Pages

| Item | Count | URLs |
|---|---|---|

### Reused

- ShopLite (D1).
- The mock API (P07 L15).
- The JS engine (E1, from W5, or built early in W4 if W5 has not run; W4 then depends on that part).
- The lab shell.

### New

1. **E7 Locator evaluator** (`engines/locator/`)
   - **Supported:** `getByRole(role, { name, exact, checked, level })`, `getByLabel`, `getByText(exact or substring)`, `getByPlaceholder`, `getByAltText`, `getByTitle`, `getByTestId`, CSS `locator()`, `.filter({ hasText })`, `.first()`, `.nth()`.
   - **Role mapping:** implicit HTML roles for about 30 elements; accessible name from aria-labelledby, aria-label, `<label>`, alt, title and text content.
   - **Feedback:** matches highlighted in the ShopLite iframe; a count; "strict-mode violation" when there is more than one match and the task needs one.
   - **Stated limit:** "a teaching subset of Playwright's locator rules".
2. **Static and simulated code checker** for Playwright test code (TS or JS)
   - **Tokenizer and rules:** awaits on actions and assertions; no `waitForTimeout`; prefers role and label locators; assertions use `expect(locator)` web-first forms; test isolation (no shared mutable state); page-object usage where required.
   - **Simulated run:** executes the supported API subset against a ShopLite DOM model in the JS worker (goto, click, fill, check, selectOption, the expect matchers listed in P07 L6).
   - **Labelling:** every task is labelled "Checked in your browser by a simulation; run the starter project for a real run".

### Verification

Every P07 task's reference solution is run with **real Playwright locally** against ShopLite (the starter project) at the stated version; the result is recorded in the lesson's `reviewed`.

### Content volume

19 lessons, about 60 checked tasks, 25 interview questions and 1 project with 5 milestones.

### UI specifics

- **Desktop:** a three-panel layout for locator lessons (roadmap collapsed, lesson, ShopLite panel).
- **Phone:** a ShopLite preview above the editor, with a "Show matches" toggle.

### Acceptance and sanity checks

- The locator evaluator's results equal real Playwright's on a fixed set of 40 locator cases against ShopLite (recorded locally); any mismatch documents the subset or fixes the evaluator.
- The starter zip runs `npx playwright test` green on stable and red on `r-bugs-1` for the bug-catching tests (recorded).

### Dependencies, effort and risks

- **Dependencies:** D1 ShopLite and the mock API; the E1 JS engine.
- **Effort:** locator evaluator 2, code checker 2, starter project 1, content 4, tool page 1; **about 10**.
- **Risks:**
  - Playwright API drift: a version note on every page; review on minor releases.
  - Accuracy of the evaluator vs real Playwright: the 40-case comparison.

### Reused

The Text Diff and List Comparer engines (git diffs), the lab shell, and the JSON engines (P30 objects and JSON).

### New

1. **E7 Git simulator** (`engines/git/`)
   - **Model:** an object store (commit = { id, parents[], tree: { path: content }, message }), refs, HEAD, index, working tree, a remote `origin` (a second model).
   - **Commands:** [07](07-interactive-tools-catalogue.md) E7.
   - **Conflicts:** a line-based three-way merge produces conflict markers; the conflict editor offers "keep ours, keep theirs, keep both or edit".
   - **Graph:** an SVG commit graph laid out by topological order, branch lanes and ref labels.
   - **Accessibility:** a text list alternative.
   - **Goal checker:** a topology and ref comparison (GS).
   - **Mobile from the start** (Learn Git Branching is not; [17](17-competitor-experience-audit.md), section 3):
     - a terminal input with suggestion chips for commands;
     - the graph scrolls horizontally inside its box;
     - files in a tab.
2. **E1 JS engine** (`engines/js/`)
   - **Runtime:** a dedicated Worker; the learner code runs as a Blob module; console captured; a 3 s limit.
   - **Test helper:** `expect` in the worker.
   - **Error explanations:** for the 15 most common errors (ReferenceError, TypeError: undefined is not a function, SyntaxError: unexpected token, and so on).
3. **E1 Python engine** (W6, after D3)
   - Pyodide vendored with a VERSION file and licence.
   - "Load Python (about N MB)" button, with the measured size.
   - Runs in a Worker; stdout captured; a 10 s first-run allowance; the friendly error map for 15 common errors.

### Content volume (W5)

26 lessons, 4 projects, 45 interview questions and about 90 checked tasks.

### Acceptance and sanity checks

- Every git goal is reachable, the reference command sequence passes, and a wrong sequence gives a specific difference.
- JS: timeouts recover and console output is captured.
- Python: the first-run size and time are measured on a phone emulation and recorded on the page.

### Dependencies, effort and risks

- **Effort:** git engine 3, JS engine 1, P13 content 3, P30 content 3, tool pages 2 (W5 about 12); Python engine 2 plus content 5 (W6 about 7).
- **Risks:**
  - Pyodide size on phones: the X5 experiment.
  - Git model edge cases: limit to the supported commands and say so.

**Open decisions:** D3 (Python), D4 (Java).

## D6 AI and Machine Learning (`/practice-labs/ai-ml/`)

### Scope

W7: P60 AI and LLM Foundations, P61 Prompt Engineering and Evaluation, P06 Testing AI Systems (home D1). W8: P62 LLM Applications, P63 ML Foundations, P65 Responsible AI. W9: P64 Deep Learning.

### Hard constraints

- No runtime AI calls, no keys, no cloud AI (rule 1.5; the owner).
- Browser models only after D5.

### New

1. **E10 Prompt Evaluation Lab** (`engines/prompt-eval/`, standalone `/tools/prompt-evaluation-lab/`)
   - **Scenario format:** `{ task, context, promptVersions[], recordedOutputs: [{ promptVersion, input, output, recordedOn, modelNote: "recorded example; model and date stated" }], referenceScores, risks[] }`.
   - **Exercises:**
     - score outputs on a rubric;
     - design a rubric that covers the stated risks;
     - write test inputs that would expose a failure (checked by category coverage: edge input, injection attempt, ambiguous request, out-of-scope);
     - choose the better prompt version with reasons.
   - **Checks:** scores within ±1 of the reference per criterion; coverage of the required risk categories.
2. **E8 visualizers** (plain JS, deterministic):
   - a teaching tokenizer (a stated simplified BPE; never claims to match a vendor's);
   - a temperature and top-p sampling demo over a fixed next-token distribution;
   - an embedding similarity map (precomputed 2-D points, cosine similarity on precomputed small vectors);
   - an agent-loop simulator (plan, tool call, observation, a step budget, failure states);
   - ML visualizers (W8): linear regression with gradient descent, k-means, a decision-tree split, a confusion matrix with a threshold slider.

### Content rules

- Every recorded output is dated and states that it is recorded.
- Claims about specific models' capabilities are avoided or dated with a source.
- Each AI page has a review date of 6 months or less ([10](10-content-quality-standard.md), section 5).

### Volume

P60 about 12 lessons, P61 about 12, P06 about 10; 6 evaluation scenarios.

### Acceptance

- No network request to any AI service (Network panel check).
- Visualizers are deterministic: the same input gives the same output (a unit test).
- Every recorded output has a date.

### Effort and risks

- **Effort (W7):** lab 3, visualizers 2, content 6; **about 11**.
- **Risk:** content ageing fastest of all departments (mitigated by review dates). There is a credibility risk if AI content is shallow; mitigate with evaluation-focused, practical content rather than hype.

**Open decisions:** D5.
