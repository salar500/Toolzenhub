### Hard rejection

- **GST (runner-up):** the strongest recurring use of the remaining tools, and a natural companion to Margin and Profit, but it is essentially
  one two-way formula (add or remove a rate). Its useful extras (an intra- or inter-state split, the tax share of an inclusive price) are
  small, and any rate presets or category rates would be a rule the site must maintain. It stays Coming Soon and is the better pick if a
  stronger design (for example, an invoice-level flow) is found.
- **ROI:** one obvious formula; the time dimension pulls in annualised return (CAGR) and payback, which must not be merged in; chosen only
  to complete a Business trio, which is not a user reason.
- **CAGR:** "start, end, years in; one rate out", with low repeat use.
- **PPF and Income Tax:** rule-bound with no versioning workflow: a rate, limits, lock-in rules (PPF); slabs, regimes and years (Income Tax).
- **Health:** a result that can be read as a diagnosis, with formula and demographic choices that change the answer.
- **Interest, Personal Loan:** a generic formula and an EMI wrapper. **Loan Eligibility:** a lender's decision, and it overlaps Home Loan.
- **Math and Converter utilities:** commoditized, one obvious answer. **Currency:** needs a live rate feed.
- **No remaining Loans tool deserves Pack 7.** Loans has five live tools. Those three entries stay Coming Soon, unchanged.

### Why FD wins

1. **A real decision, not a subtraction.** People hold or renew fixed deposits and are quoted different rates for different tenures by
   different banks. The question "which offer gives me more, and how do I compare a higher rate for a shorter tenure with a lower rate
   for a longer one?" is a recurring comparison, which a one-line formula does not answer.
2. **The honest comparison measure.** Showing the **effective annual yield** next to the maturity amount lets two offers with different
   compounding or tenure be compared fairly, a point most FD pages do not make.
3. **Deterministic and evergreen.** The rate, tenure and compounding are the visitor's inputs; there is no rule, tax table or live feed
   to maintain, and the model is exact (see "Model").
4. **It deepens Investment with something SIP is not.** SIP is open-ended and assumption-driven; FD is contractual and comparative. They
   relate to each other, and the Investment cluster gains a second distinct tool.
5. **Distinct content.** Two articles with different ideas (compounding frequency, comparing offers) that do not depend on a rate quote.

### Answers to the product questions

- **Who:** an adult with savings who is renewing a fixed deposit or has been quoted rates by two banks; also family members comparing for parents.
- **Trigger:** a maturing FD, a new quote, or a relative asking "which is better"; they have two rates and two tenures in front of them.
- **Problem:** what each offer matures to, how much interest it earns, and how the two compare once tenure and compounding differ.
- **Decision after the result:** which offer to ask the bank about, or whether the difference is large enough to matter.
- **Return:** each renewal, each new quote, a different amount or tenure, a second family member's deposit.
- **Beyond the formula:** a side-by-side with the effective annual yield and the difference, and the compounding convention stated and exact.
- **Trust risk:** a deposit's return is contractual, but the page must not call any option "better", "safe" or "guaranteed", and must not
  imply a bank's quote or terms (premature withdrawal, tax on interest, payout options) are included.
- **Maintenance:** none beyond the code.

## Trust standard

- **Facts the visitor enters:** the deposit amount, the rate and tenure they were quoted. **Assumptions:** the compounding frequency (their
  choice; the page says quarterly is only an example), that interest is added to the deposit (cumulative) and not paid out, that the rate
  holds for the whole tenure, and the part-period convention below. **Calculated outputs:** maturity amount, interest earned, effective annual
  yield, the share of the deposit, and the comparison.
- **Exclusions (shown next to the result):** tax on interest and TDS, premature withdrawal and penalties, payout options (monthly or
  quarterly interest), a bank's own rounding, deposit insurance and the bank's terms. A bank's maturity figure can differ slightly.
- **Banned claims:** "best FD", "better" or "worse" offer, "safe", "risk-free", "guaranteed return", "highest return", "recommended bank or
  tenure", "you will earn", any named bank or rate shown as current, any advice to choose an option.
- **Wording for a comparison:** "Option B matures to ₹X more than Option A, with a lower/higher effective yield and a longer/shorter
  tenure." Differences are stated, never ranked.
- **Rates are examples:** the default 7% and quarterly compounding are labelled as examples, not as current or typical rates.
- **Local only:** nothing leaves the browser.

## Spec

### 1. Tool identity
FD Calculator; `fd`; Calculators, Investment; no subcategory; `toolType` calculator. Keep the catalog title "FD Calculator". New description
(replaces "Calculate fixed deposit returns."): "Work out what a fixed deposit matures to for a rate and tenure you enter, see its effective

**Expected breadcrumb:** Home › Calculators › investment › FD Calculator (the category id, styled by the shared breadcrumb, as for SIP).

### 2. User problem
**This tool helps a saver understand what a fixed deposit matures to, and how two offers with different rates, tenures or compounding
compare, when renewing a deposit or choosing between bank quotes.** Today they use a calculator that returns one maturity figure, or compare
quoted rates directly, which hides the effect of tenure and compounding.

### 3. Jobs to be done
- What will ₹X become at r% for this tenure?
- What is the effective yield of this rate and compounding?
- Offer A is 7.1% for 3 years and offer B is 6.8% for 5 years: what does each mature to and what is each one's yield?
- How much does the compounding frequency change the result?

### 4. Target users
Savers comparing fixed deposits. Not for choosing a bank, a tax computation, a payout schedule or a lender's loan.

### 5. Inputs
Two blocks in the shared form grid (two columns on desktop, one on mobile), a hint under each field, optional fields marked "(optional)",
Reset. The compounding control is the Margin segmented radio pattern.

### 6. Outputs and result hierarchy
Above the fold on desktop: the form, then the result block.
1. **Primary:** the maturity amount of Option A (₹, to the paisa), large.
2. **Supporting cards (always three, so the grid is a balanced two-by-two with the primary):** interest earned; effective annual yield; interest
   as a share of the deposit. Each answers a different question (how much, what rate really, how much of the deposit).
3. **Summary (one or two sentences):** "₹1,00,000 at 7% a year for 5 years, compounded quarterly, matures to ₹1,41,477.82."
4. **Trust note** beside the result.
5. **Comparison table (only when Option B has a rate):** see section 12.
6. **Next useful actions (related tools):** SIP (a different way to build a sum).
Nothing is shown that the visitor has to decode; no recommendation is made.

### 7. Model rules
Rupees; deposit P, rate r (percent a year), compounding m times a year (12, 4, 2, 1), tenure n months. Interest is added to the deposit at the end
of each completed compounding period (cumulative), and the rate is constant.
- Period rate i = r ÷ 100 ÷ m; period length = 12 ÷ m months; completed periods k = ⌊n ÷ (12 ÷ m)⌋; remaining months q = n − k × (12 ÷ m).
- **Maturity** M = P × (1 + i)^k × (1 + i × q ÷ (12 ÷ m)). The last, part period earns **simple interest pro rata** at the period rate; with q = 0
  it is the plain compound formula. (Banks and calculators differ on part periods and rounding; the page says so.)
- **Interest earned** = M − P. **Share of the deposit** = (M − P) ÷ P.
- **Effective annual yield** = (1 + i)^m − 1 (the yearly growth of the deposit with the compounding chosen; equal to r for yearly compounding).
- **Comparison:** the same P and compounding with Option B's rate and tenure; the difference is B − A for maturity, interest and yield. When the
  tenures differ the page says the longer deposit has had longer to grow and that the yields are the like-for-like measure; it makes no statement
  about what happens after the shorter one matures.
- **Precision:** exact arithmetic (whole-number arithmetic on the typed decimals, the power (1 + i)^k kept as an exact ratio, k up to 360, as the Home
  Loan engine does for its power); money handed back as numbers, shown to the paisa. Percentages to 2 decimals. Nothing is rounded in the model.
- **Independent verification:** section 22.

### 8. Assumptions (shown on the page)
The rate and compounding are the visitor's; the rate is constant for the whole tenure; interest is added to the deposit and not paid out; a
part period earns simple interest pro rata. Not included: tax on interest and TDS, premature withdrawal, payout options, a bank's rounding and
terms, deposit insurance. Another calculator or a bank may give a slightly different figure. A calculation, not a bank's quote or advice.

### 9. Edge cases
- Rate 0%: maturity equals the deposit; interest 0; yield 0%.
- Tenure 1 month; tenure under one compounding period (all part-period simple interest); tenure of exactly k periods (no part period).
- Yearly compounding: the effective yield equals the rate.
- The largest valid values (₹10 crore, 20%, 30 years, monthly): a very large maturity, shown in full with Indian grouping.
- Option B with the same terms as A: a difference of ₹0 and equal yields, stated plainly.
- Option B with a blank tenure (the same as A) and with a different tenure.
- Option B's rate blank: no comparison. Option B tenure entered without a rate: ignored (a comparison needs a rate); the hint says so.

### 10. Validation
Limits and messages as in section 5, tool-owned, with the shared field helpers for linked errors (`aria-invalid`, `aria-describedby`). An
emptied required field shows the prompt, not an error; an emptied optional field means none. Total tenure 1 to 360 months. Nothing negative.

### 11. User journey
1. The visitor arrives with a quote or a maturing deposit ("what will this become?").
2. They enter the amount, rate, tenure and compounding (four decisions, one screen).
3. The maturity amount is the first thing they read, with the interest and the effective yield beside it.
4. The summary states the terms in a sentence, so they can check it matches the quote.
5. They enter a second rate (and tenure) and the comparison table appears: maturity, interest, yield and the differences.
6. The assumptions and what is not included sit beside the result.
7. They try another amount or rate, or open the SIP Calculator.

### 20. Article cluster and article visual strategy
Distinct, no forced count; none depends on a rate quote or a bank's terms. Image decisions follow "concept first, format second"; the two visuals

**Article 1: How much does compounding frequency change an FD's maturity?** (explanation and interpretation)
- *Intent:* people see "quarterly" or "monthly" and wonder if it matters. *Unique value:* on one deposit, ₹1,00,000 at 7% for 5 years matures to
  ₹1,40,255.17 yearly, ₹1,41,059.88 half-yearly, ₹1,41,477.82 quarterly and ₹1,41,762.53 monthly: monthly is ₹1,507.35 more than yearly, about
  half of what a 0.4-point higher rate does (7.4% quarterly is ₹1,44,284.83, ₹2,807.01 more than 7% quarterly). The effective yields (7.0000%,
  7.1225%, 7.1859%, 7.2290%). *Calculator relationship:* the compounding control and the effective-yield card. *Not a duplicate:* it is about one
  variable (frequency), not comparing offers.
- **IMAGE: YES.** *Visual purpose:* show that compounding means interest is added to the deposit at set moments, and that more frequent
  compounding means more such moments in a year. *Visual type:* **timeline of compounding moments**: one year drawn four times (yearly, half-yearly,
  quarterly, monthly), each row a line with a tick at every point where interest is added. *Why this type:* the idea is *when* interest is added,
  which a timeline shows directly; a chart of maturities would show four nearly equal bars and hide the point. *Why not a chart:* the differences are
  small, and the concept is structural, not a trend. *Data source:* the number of compounding moments per year (1, 2, 4, 12) from the model; no
  money values in the image. *Anti-repetition:* a row-of-ticks structural diagram, unlike the other article's visual. *Alt-text intent:* four
  stacked one-year lines with 1, 2, 4 and 12 marks where interest is added.

**Article 2: Comparing two fixed deposits: a higher rate is not the whole comparison** (scenario and decision guide)
- *Intent:* choosing between 7.1% for 3 years and 6.8% for 5 years. *Unique value:* on ₹1,00,000 compounded quarterly, A matures to ₹1,23,507.50 and
  B to ₹1,40,093.85; A's effective yield is 7.2913% and B's 6.9754%; B is higher in rupees only because it runs two years longer, and the page
  says what the numbers do and do not show (nothing about what A is reinvested in after three years). *Calculator relationship:* the Option B
  fields and the comparison table. *Not a duplicate:* it is about comparing offers, not about frequency.
- **IMAGE: YES.** *Visual purpose:* make the different lengths of the two deposits visible, which is why the maturities cannot be compared
  directly. *Visual type:* **timeline of two deposits**: two bars on one years axis (3 years and 5 years), a marker at each maturity, a bracket
  showing the two extra years that only Option B runs. *Why this type:* the key fact is duration; a bar chart of maturity values would make B look
  better and bury that point. *Why not a chart:* the image is about length of time, not magnitude of money. *Data source:* the two tenures (36 and 60
  months) from the model; no money values. *Anti-repetition:* a duration diagram with brackets, unlike the tick-row timeline of article 1 (one is
  repeated marks within a year, the other is spans across years). *Alt-text intent:* two horizontal bars of three and five years with the extra
  two years marked.

### 22. Tests (risk-based) and independent verification
**Independent reference:** `tests/fixtures/fd-golden.py` (Python `decimal`, 60 digits). It does **not** use the closed form to find the maturity: it
**simulates the deposit period by period** (balance = balance × (1 + i) for each completed period, then simple interest on the balance for the part
period) and computes the effective yield by **multiplying the growth of m periods**; the closed forms (P × (1 + i)^k × (1 + i × q ÷ period), (1 + i)^m − 1)
are computed separately and asserted equal. It recomputes every comparison difference and prints the figures quoted in the articles. The JSON is embedded as
`GOLDEN` in `tests/unit/fd-golden.test.mjs`; no figure is copied from the JavaScript.
**During implementation, run only what the change can break; never the whole site:**
- formula change: `tests/unit/fd-golden.test.mjs` only;

## Checkpoint clarifications (spec commit review)

These sharpen the sections above; where they differ, this section wins.

1. **Product scope.** FD is not "deposit + rate + tenure = maturity". The job is to compare fixed-deposit offers with different rates, tenures
   and compounding conventions and understand what each matures to: maturity, interest, effective annual yield, compounding frequency, an
   optional second offer, and a neutral account of why the outcomes differ.
2. **Option B boundary.** Option B uses the **same deposit amount** as Option A ("with the same principal, how do these two offers differ?"). V1 has
   no second amount input, no third option and no portfolio comparison. Option B's tenure blank means Option A's tenure. Exactly six inputs in v1.
3. **Third supporting card.** "Interest as a share of the deposit" means (maturity − deposit) ÷ deposit over the **whole tenure** (for example 41.48%
   on ₹1,00,000 over 5 years at 7% quarterly). It answers "how much did the deposit grow in total?" and is **not** a yearly rate; the label says "Total
   growth over the tenure" and its hint says it is not the effective annual yield and not a CAGR. It is kept because it is the only whole-tenure
   figure beside the yearly yield, not to fill the grid; if implementation finds it confusing, drop it and use a primary plus two cards in a layout
   without an orphan, rather than a filler.
4. **Effective annual yield** = (1 + r ÷ m)^m − 1 with r the nominal annual rate as a fraction and m the compounding periods a year. It is the
   full-year equivalent **under the entered compounding convention**. It is different from the nominal quoted rate, from the total growth over the tenure
   and from the CAGR of any other investment. It is never called guaranteed or "the bank's yield"; a bank's own figure can differ.
5. **Partial-period convention is a ToolZenHub modelling assumption, not a universal banking rule.** Full compounding periods compound; any remaining months
   earn **simple interest pro rata** at the period rate: M = P × (1+i)^k × (1 + i × q ÷ periodLength). Actual bank maturity may differ because banks can use
   different compounding dates, day-count conventions, deposit products, rounding rules or treatment of broken periods. This sentence appears in the
   model assumptions, the trust note on the page, the exclusions and the articles, and the page never presents the convention as how banks work.
6. **Exact-period tenures** (for example 5 years quarterly, 3 years monthly, 2 years yearly) have q = 0: pure compound interest, no pro-rata tail. **Zero
   rate:** maturity equals the deposit, interest 0, effective yield 0%, no division.
7. **Different-tenure rule (core differentiator).** When Option A and Option B have different tenures, the page shows beside the table: "The two deposits
   run for different lengths of time, so their maturity amounts are not a like-for-like comparison. Compare the effective annual yields together with the
   tenures." A larger maturity over a longer tenure is never described as superior (₹1,40,094 over 5 years is not "more" than ₹1,23,508 over 3 years in
   any ranking sense). Differences are worded "Option B matures to ₹X more/less than Option A under these inputs", never "better", "winner" or "recommended".
8. **Table question and rows.** "How do these two FD offers differ under the same deposit amount?" Rows: interest rate, tenure, compounding (shared, one
   value), maturity, interest earned, effective annual yield; Difference (B − A) column; no highlighting of a row or cell as best.
9. **No advice.** The tool never recommends or ranks a bank, product, rate or tenure, never says an FD is safe or risk-free, and never guarantees maturity.
10. **Non-scope (confirmed, v1):** live rates, bank names and rankings, deposit-insurance calculations, tax, TDS, premature-withdrawal penalties,
    interest-payout FDs (monthly or quarterly payout), senior-citizen rates, callable or sweep products, a third option, a different Option B principal,
    day-level tenure, a target-maturity reverse solver, saved comparisons, Print, CSV, chart.
11. **Trust additions.** Facts entered: deposit, quoted rate, tenure. Assumptions: compounding frequency (chosen), constant rate, interest stays invested,
    the partial-period convention. Calculated: maturity, interest, yield, comparison differences. Excluded: tax, TDS, premature withdrawal, payout variants,
    bank-specific rounding, day-count rules, product terms, deposit insurance, changing rates. Banned claims: best, safest, safe, risk-free, guaranteed,
    recommended, highest-return product, any named-bank recommendation, any current market rate.
12. **Goldens coverage.** The table in section 23 covers: default quarterly (A), monthly (A2), half-yearly (A3), yearly (A4), exact period (C), partial
    period (B, D), zero rate (E), smallest amount (F), largest amount (G, ₹38,39,63,96,323.27 in Indian grouping), shortest tenure (F, 1 month), longest
    tenure (G, 360 months), Option B same tenure (I), Option B different tenure (H), identical options (J). At implementation the reference adds an exact-period
    5-year quarterly case if not covered. Every value is re-derived by `tests/fixtures/fd-golden.py` (Python Decimal, high precision), which steps through the
    periods, applies the explicit convention, computes the effective yield by multiplying period growth, computes Option B separately and prints the article
    figures; it must not be a line-for-line translation of the JavaScript.
13. **Articles (titles as approved).** (1) How Much Does Compounding Frequency Change an FD's Maturity? (2) Comparing Two Fixed Deposits: A Higher Rate Is Not
    the Whole Comparison. Candidate only, not built: What an FD Calculation Leaves Out. No filler third article.
14. **Visuals (concept first, format second).** Article 1: a structural timeline of compounding moments **within one year** (yearly 1, half-yearly 2,
    quarterly 4, monthly 12 interest-addition moments); not four maturity bars, not a line chart, not a histogram. Article 2: a **span comparison of total
    durations** (3 years against 5 years, the extra 2 years highlighted); not a "which bar is taller" maturity chart. The compositions must be visually
    distinct: article 1 is four stacked one-year rows of tick marks (frequency within a period); article 2 is two horizontal spans of unequal length with a
    bracket (different total durations). No money values in either image. If implementation finds an image adds no explanatory value, NO IMAGE is acceptable.
    Images are generated only in implementation, never in this checkpoint.
15. **SEO title.** The draft title may be too long. At implementation the final title may be shortened to follow the site's title conventions while keeping
