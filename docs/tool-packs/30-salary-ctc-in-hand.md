### 0.2 Verified findings

1. **[VERIFIED] Wage ceiling (S1).** S.O. 5109(E), "in exercise of the powers conferred by clause (89) of section 2 of the Code on Social Security, 2020", supersedes S.O. 2702(E) of 29 May 2026 (except things done before) and "notifies rupees twenty-five thousand (25,000) per month as the wage ceiling for the purposes of Chapter III of the said Code, **with effect from the date of publication** in the Official Gazette". Publication date: 17 September 2026. So the ceiling is ₹25,000 per month from 17 September 2026.
2. **[VERIFIED] What a "wage ceiling" is (S3).** Section 2(89): "such amount of wages as may be notified by the Central Government, for the purposes of becoming a member under Chapter III and Chapter IV". It is a **membership-eligibility** threshold. The notification itself says nothing about contribution amounts.
3. **[VERIFIED] The ceiling does not limit the Provident Fund Scheme's "employee" (S3, section 2(26) first proviso).** For Chapter III "**except in case of the Employees' Provident Fund Scheme** and Chapter IV", an employee means one drawing wages at or below the wage ceiling. The statute therefore keeps the Provident Fund Scheme outside that proviso; how the ceiling limits **contributions** is set by the scheme text, read in Phase 3 (items 10 and 11).
4. **[VERIFIED] Contribution rate (S2, S3).** Section 16(1)(a): the employer pays 10% of wages, the employee's contribution equals the employer's and "may, if any employee so desires, be an amount exceeding" it, and the employer is not obliged to match more. S.O. 3582(E) (1 July 2026, deemed in force from 21 November 2025) specifies **twelve per cent** for the employer and employees of establishments covered under section 1(4), (5) and (6) of the Code, "of Employees' Provident Funds Scheme, 2026", except: establishments under an approved insolvency resolution or repayment plan, and the jute, beedi, brick, coir (non-spinning) and guar-gum industries (those stay at the statutory 10%).
5. **[VERIFIED] Pension share (S3).** Section 16(1)(b)(i): the sums paid into the Pension Fund from the employer's contribution are "not exceeding eight and one-third per cent of the wages **or such per cent of wages as may be notified**".
6. **[VERIFIED] Definition of wages for the Social Security Code (S3, section 2(88)).** "All remuneration ... includes basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance"; excludes, among others, employer contributions to a pension or provident fund (c), house rent allowance (f), overtime (h), commission (i), gratuity payable on termination (j) and retrenchment or other retirement benefits (k); and the **first proviso**: where payments under sub-clauses (a) to (i) exceed one-half, or such other percentage as notified, of all remuneration, the excess "shall be deemed as remuneration and shall be accordingly added in wages".
7. **[PROVISIONAL] What the proviso counts (S5, official clarification).** Total remuneration for the 50% test: employer PF and pension contributions and statutory bonus are included; gratuity, ESI and other retirement benefits are **not**; "performance based incentives, ESOPs, variable part of the component or reimbursement-based payments ... shall not be part of the wages"; overtime forms part of the test. The ministry's illustration: remuneration ₹76,000, basic ₹20,000, allowances ₹40,000, gratuity and retrenchment ₹16,000; the allowance cap is 50% of ₹76,000 = ₹38,000; ₹2,000 is added back; statutory wages become ₹22,000. This is a clarification, not statute.
8. **[PROVISIONAL, now consistent with the Gazette texts in items 1, 10 and 12] Press statements on the ceiling (S4).** The ceiling for mandatory EPFO coverage rises from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month with effect from 17 September 2026 (the Cabinet decision was 16 September); employees "joining an establishment at wages above Rs. 15,000 ... are not automatically covered" under the old arrangement; EPFO officers state that, for employees whose contributions were restricted to the ₹15,000 ceiling, "the wage ceiling for monthly contributions shall be enhanced to ₹25,000 per month **or the actual salary/wages, whichever is lower**, subject to the applicable statutory provisions and scheme rules"; and the maximum monthly employer pension contribution at 8.33% moves from ₹1,250 to ₹2,083 (8.33% of ₹25,000). The contribution-basis statement came from a regional press meet; the scheme text (item 10) now confirms the capped base.
9. **[VERIFIED, carried forward from TP17] Tax for tax year 2026-27 (S8).** Income-tax Act, 2025 in force from 1 April 2026; new-regime slabs (nil to ₹4 lakh, then 5% to ₹8L, 10% to ₹12L, 15% to ₹16L, 20% to ₹20L, 25% to ₹24L, 30% above); old-regime slabs (under 60) nil to ₹2.5L, 5% to ₹5L, 20% to ₹10L, 30% above; standard deduction ₹75,000 (new, s.19(1) Table Sl. 2 with s.202(1)) and ₹50,000 (old); rebate s.156 (new: total income up to ₹12 lakh, lesser of tax or ₹60,000, with marginal relief; old: up to ₹5 lakh, up to ₹12,500); 4% cess; professional tax deductible from salary in the old regime and disallowed in the new (s.19(1) Table Sl. 1; s.202(2)(a)(iv)); 80C-type cap ₹1,50,000 (s.123); rounding to the nearest ten rupees (s.516). Supported taxpayer: resident individual under 60, salary plus other income up to ₹50 lakh, so surcharge cannot apply. TP17's configuration is valid 2026-04-01 to 2027-03-31.

10. **[VERIFIED] The contribution base is capped at the wage ceiling (S11, paragraph 18).** 18(2): the employer's contribution is "twelve per cent of the wages payable to the employee" (ten per cent for classes of establishment notified by the Central Government) and "the employees' contribution shall be equal to the employer's". 18(3): "The contribution payable in respect of a member shall be subject to the wage ceiling limit, notified by the Central Government ... where the monthly wage of such a member exceeds the wage ceiling, the employer and employee's contribution shall be limited to the contribution payable on the wage ceiling" (subject to paragraph 9(4) and a pension-fund proviso). 18(4): contributions are calculated on wages "actually drawn or payable during the month". **18(5): each contribution is calculated to the nearest rupee, fifty paise or more counting up.**
11. **[VERIFIED] Contribution above the ceiling is optional, not statutory (S11).** Paragraph 2(f)(i): an "excluded employee" is one whose wage "at the time he is otherwise entitled to become a member of the Fund, exceeds wage ceiling". Paragraph 9(4): an employee and employer "may jointly opt in writing" to enrol such an employee "or allow him to contribute on such wages exceeding wage ceiling limit"; the employer pays the administrative charges. Paragraph 19(1) to (4): an employee "may opt to contribute on a voluntary basis, an additional contribution on wages exceeding the statutory wage ceiling at statutory rate or at any rate in excess of statutory rate"; the employer "may if he so desires" match but is "under no obligation to match"; the employer is liable for additional administrative charges on those wages; either side may reduce or stop at any time. Paragraph 9(1): every member of the 1952 scheme continues as a member. **Consequence:** "12% of the whole basic" is a voluntary or jointly agreed arrangement; it is not the statutory base; and a person whose wage exceeded the ceiling when first entitled to join may not be a member at all.
12. **[VERIFIED] Pension share and the two different "15,000" and "25,000" figures (S12).** Paragraph 4(1): "a part of contribution of eight and thirty-three hundredths per cent. of the wages of employee up to wage ceiling notified by the Central Government" goes to the Pension Fund, so the maximum is 8.33% of the notified ceiling (₹2,083 a month at ₹25,000, matching the PIB statement). Paragraph 4(2): the **Central Government's own** contribution of 1.16% of pay; G.S.R. 890(E) (15 October 2026, in force from 17 September 2026) replaces "such sum as may be specified by the Central Government" with "wages upto rupees fifteen thousand (Rs. 15,000/-) per month" and omits "and the Central Government" from the first proviso. That ₹15,000 limit therefore governs the Government's share, not the employer's or employee's, and it does not enter this calculator. G.S.R. 847(E) (25 September 2026) inserts a membership category into paragraph 7(1). The split of the employer's 12% between pension and provident fund changes no employee figure and no CTC figure, so the calculator does not show it.
13. **[VERIFIED] Employer-contribution tax threshold (S13, section 17(1)(h)).** The Income-tax Act, 2025 treats as a perquisite "aggregate amount of any contribution, in excess of ₹7,50,000 in a tax year, made to the account of the assessee by the employer" in a recognised provident fund, in the section 124(1) scheme and in an approved superannuation fund; section 17(1)(i) adds the annual accretion on that excess. With the P0 CTC cap of ₹50,00,000 the largest possible employer provident-fund contribution is 12% of CTC = ₹6,00,000, below ₹7,50,000, so P0 does not need to model it. Employer NPS is not in P0.
14. **[VERIFIED] Tax provisions re-read on 2026-10-22 (S13).** Section 202(1) rate table: nil to ₹4,00,000; 5% to ₹8,00,000; 10% to ₹12,00,000; and so on up to 30% above ₹24,00,000. Section 156(1): old-regime rebate where total income does not exceed ₹5,00,000, 100% of the tax or ₹12,500, whichever is less. Section 156(2): new-regime rebate to ₹12,00,000 (lesser of the tax or ₹60,000) with marginal relief above. Section 19(1) Table Sl. 2: standard deduction ₹75,000 or the salary, whichever is less, where tax is computed under section 202(1), and ₹50,000 otherwise. These match TP17's configuration. **Not re-read in Phase 3:** the Finance Act, 2026 old-regime slabs and cess, the Income-tax Rules, 2026 rule on HRA, and sections 123, 124 and 126; TP17's earlier reading of them stands (S8) and is re-verified at build time.
15. **[VERIFIED, S3 start-of-phase check] Tax provisions that Phase 3 did not re-read, now read in primary text (2026-10-22).** Finance Act, 2026 (`egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/271439.pdf`): First Schedule Part I-B Paragraph A(I), nil to ₹2,50,000, 5% to ₹5,00,000, ₹12,500 plus 20% to ₹10,00,000, ₹1,12,500 plus 30% above; and the "Health and Education Cess on income-tax" at four per cent of income-tax and surcharge. Income-tax Rules, 2026 (`incometax.gov.in`, notified 20 March 2026), rule 279: the HRA exemption is the least of the allowance received, rent paid above one-tenth of salary, and 50% (Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi, Chennai, Hyderabad, Pune, Ahmedabad, Bengaluru) or 40% of salary, "salary" including dearness allowance where the terms provide it. Income-tax Act, 2025: section 123 (deduction up to ₹1,50,000 for the Schedule XV sums, **Schedule XV (f) includes "contribution by an employee to a recognised provident fund"**, so employee PF counts as an 80C-type amount) and section 124(1) and (2) (employer pension-scheme contribution). All agree with Tool Pack 17's configuration; nothing differs.
16. **[VERIFIED negative result, S3] No percentage other than one-half is set in the Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026.** The Rules (G.S.R. 343(E), 8 May 2026, eGazette ID 272365) were read for any percentage, "one-half" or "fifty per cent": the only fifty per cent provisions are the limits on deductions and recoveries. Together with the Social Security (Central) Rules, 2026 (0.1 S7), **no notified percentage appears in either set of Rules**. A separate Gazette notification under the proviso is not excluded: the eGazette portal cannot be enumerated by machine, so the absence of one is **not proved**.
17. **[NOT PROVED, S3] Amendments to the EPF and Pension Schemes and to the ceiling and rate notifications after the specification's source freeze.** A web search found none after G.S.R. 890(E) (15 October 2026); the EPFO circulars page lists no 2026 item; eGazette cannot be enumerated. The formula inputs (12%, ₹25,000, monthly rounding) rest on the Gazette texts read in Phase 3, and no source read contradicts them, so this is a standing uncertainty, not a contradiction. The configuration records `verifiedOn` 2026-10-22.

### 0.3 Evidence conflicts and caveats (reported, not resolved by choosing a convenient reading)

1. **Membership ceiling against contribution base: RESOLVED by primary text.** The Gazette fixes a membership ceiling (S1, S3); the Scheme limits the mandatory contribution to the wage ceiling (S11, 18(3)) and makes anything above it voluntary or jointly agreed (S11, 9(4) and 19). Commentary and the EPFO regional press briefing agree with this.
2. **A mid-year change.** The ceiling changed on 17 September 2026, part-way through tax year 2026-27. S1 says "with effect from the date of publication" and gives no split-month rule, and S11 18(4) calculates contributions on the wages of "the month". The calculator therefore models a **run-rate at the current ceiling** and says so (D4).
3. **Reports that the pension wage ceiling is ₹15,000.** Some summaries of G.S.R. 890(E) say the pension ceiling was "fixed at ₹15,000". The Gazette text shows the figure limits the **Central Government's** 1.16% contribution under paragraph 4(2). The employer's 8.33% is on wages up to the notified ceiling (paragraph 4(1)). Reported here because a reader could misapply the ₹15,000 to the employee's contribution.
4. **Code on Wages against the Code on Social Security.** The owner pointed to Code on Wages section 2(y); the text read here is the parallel definition in section 2(88) of the Code on Social Security, the one that governs provident fund wages. The ministry FAQs describe one definition across the four Codes. The 2(y) text itself was not obtainable (S6).
5. **Employer-only charges.** Commentary disagrees on EDLI and administrative charge rates. They are employer cost, not employee deductions, and are not modelled; they enter only through the user's "other employer costs" amount. The EDLI Scheme, 2026 (G.S.R. 526(E)) was not read. **[OUT OF SCOPE]**
6. **Mixed percentage in the 12% notification.** S.O. 3582(E) specifies twelve per cent but excludes a few industries and insolvency-plan establishments; those stay at ten per cent (S11 18(2) first proviso).

## 1. Tool identity

- **Public name (proposed):** Salary / CTC to In-Hand Calculator (India).
- **Id and route (proposed, D8):** `salary-in-hand`, `/calculators/salary-in-hand/`; Calculators, Tax. Confirm that the id is unused at implementation.
- **Product job:** "Given my annual CTC and how my employer builds my salary, what is my estimated take-home pay?" It explains **what is employer cost and not cash**, what is deducted from the employee, and the estimated income tax. Decision support, not a payslip, not payroll advice.
- **Why it still matters in the AI era (rulebook 1.4):** an exact, deterministic, sourced breakdown that reconciles to the rupee, on a tax engine with 238 independent golden cases, without sending salary data anywhere.
- **Not** an individual payslip, a TDS statement or a tax-filing tool.

## 2. User problem, jobs and users

- **Problem:** CTC, gross, taxable income and take-home are different numbers; offers quote CTC; the cash arriving each month is lower and employer-dependent.
- **Jobs:** (1) compare an offer's CTC with expected take-home; (2) see what happens in the new and old regimes; (3) understand why gross and CTC differ; (4) sanity-check a revised salary.
- **Users:** salaried employees in India under 60, in an organised-sector job, resident, with one employer for the year.
- **Repeat use:** occasional (offers, appraisals). A **hypothesis**; no demand or retention data exists.

## 3. Scope and exclusions

**P0 [PROPOSED]**

- Annual CTC (₹); basic salary as % of CTC and the PF basis (both **required explicit entries, no defaults**: D1, D2); the gratuity-in-CTC choice; optional other employer-only costs; optional professional tax (annual ₹, visible, default 0).
- New-regime estimated take-home as the primary result (annual, and average monthly).
- **[DECIDED, D6]** A qualified, **optional** old-regime comparison, shown only when the user switches it on and supplies its applicable inputs (6.6). It uses the Tool Pack 17 engine unchanged, lists the deductions and exemptions included and excluded, gives no "better regime" verdict, and is never presented as a personal tax assessment.
- The tax shown is the **estimated annual income-tax liability**, not monthly tax deducted at source (6.5).
- Breakdown: employer cost not paid in cash, employee deductions, tax, take-home; visible assumptions, tax year, "verified on" date and sources.
- Automatic results, Reset, stale-result safeguards (rulebook 7.9).

**P1 [PROPOSED]** state professional-tax table (after official gazettes are read), variable pay and bonus handling, two-offer comparison, copy summary, editable provident-fund and gratuity amounts, an informational note on the 50% rule (after a legal wording review), voluntary PF above 12%.

**P2 / excluded [OUT OF SCOPE]** payslip-grade modelling, ESI, LTA and flexible-benefit plans, ESOPs, joining or leaving mid-year, multiple employers, arrears, employer NPS (TP17 supports it; not in P0), incomes above TP17's ceiling, surcharge, seniors, non-residents, other income, house-property loss, currency conversion, share links, saved data.

**Input cap for P0 [PROPOSED]:** annual CTC up to **₹50,00,000**, TP17's supported ceiling. With CTC at most ₹50 lakh the employer provident-fund contribution (at most 12% of CTC, ₹6,00,000) stays below the ₹7,50,000 aggregate threshold of section 17(1)(h) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (**[VERIFIED]**, 0.2 item 13), so P0 does not model it. A CTC above the cap is refused with TP17's wording style, never approximated.

## 5. Outputs and what each figure means

| Figure | Meaning | Is it cash to the employee? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual CTC | Employer's total yearly cost of employing the person, as the employer quotes it | n/a (cost) |
| Employer PF (EPF plus EPS share) | Employer's contribution to the provident and pension funds | No |
| Gratuity allowance | Employer's accrual towards gratuity, if the CTC includes it | No (payable on leaving, under conditions) |
| Other employer-only costs | As entered | No |
| **Gross salary** | CTC minus the three lines above: the salary the employer pays the employee before employee deductions | Before deductions |
| Employee PF | The employee's own provident fund contribution | Deducted |
| Professional tax | As entered | Deducted |
| Estimated annual income tax | The Tool Pack 17 engine result for the regime shown, including cess: the **estimated liability for the year**, not the employer's monthly withholding (TDS) | Deducted over the year; timing set by the employer |
| **Estimated annual take-home** | Gross salary minus employee PF, professional tax and estimated income tax | Yes |

Distinctions the page must keep: employer cost, gross salary, taxable income (the engine's total income, shown for transparency only), employee deductions, estimated take-home. The page must never describe a CTC component as cash unless it is part of gross salary.

**Three things the page must not blur [DECIDED refinement, Phase 4]:** (1) the **estimated annual income-tax liability** this tool computes; (2) the **tax deducted at source (TDS)** an employer withholds month by month, which follows the employer's own projection, declarations and timing and may be uneven or differ from (1); this tool does not model it; (3) the **average monthly take-home**, which is the annual estimate divided by 12 and is not an exact future payslip.

## 6. Calculation and model rules [PROPOSED; D1, D2, D6, D11 and D12 are DECIDED and applied here]

**6.0 Notation and configuration.** All money is in whole rupees (integers). Intermediate values are exact rationals (the oracle uses `Fraction`; the module must not use binary floating point for money). `rhu(x)` is round half up to the whole rupee for a non-negative exact value, `floor(x + 1/2)`. A Tool Pack 30 configuration object, separate from Tool Pack 17's, holds with `validFrom`, `validThrough`, `verifiedOn` and sources: the provident fund rate `R` = 12% (S2; S11 18(2)); the monthly wage ceiling `P` = ₹25,000, in force from 17 September 2026 (S1); the gratuity fraction 15/312 of annual basic (the 15/26 days-per-year convention divided by 12, an employer convention, 0.4 item 6); the CTC cap ₹50,00,000 (D10). A `validThrough` tripwire is matched to Tool Pack 17's behaviour at build time.

**6.1 Order** (annual figures; `P` is monthly):

1. `basic = rhu(CTC × basicPct / 100)`. `basicPct` is entered with at most two decimals and held as integer hundredths, so `basic = rhu(CTC × hundredths / 10000)`. "Basic" means basic salary including dearness allowance (the label says so, D1); the same figure is passed to the tax engine as `basicPlusDa`.
2. `monthlyBasic = basic / 12`, an exact rational that is **not** rounded: the model treats the annual basic as paid in twelve equal months.
3. PF base per month: `capped`: `min(monthlyBasic, P)`; `full-basic`: `monthlyBasic`; `none`: 0.
4. `monthlyPF = rhu(R × PFbase)` (EPF Scheme 2026 paragraph 18(5): each contribution to the nearest rupee, fifty paise counting up); `employerPF = 12 × monthlyPF`; `employeePF = employerPF` (18(2)). Employer PF here is the employer's whole 12%, the provident and the pension share together (0.2 item 12).
5. `gratuity = rhu(basic × 15 / 312)` if the gratuity choice is yes, else 0 ([PROVISIONAL]: employer convention, shown as an assumption).
6. `gross = CTC − employerPF − gratuity − otherEmployerCosts`. Refuse if `gross <= 0` or `gross < basic`.
7. Tax (section 7): the new-regime figure `taxNew` always; the old-regime figure `taxOld` only under 6.6.
8. `annualTakeHome(regime) = gross − employeePF − professionalTax − tax(regime)`. Refuse the result (no figure) if it is negative.
9. `monthlyAverage = rhu(annualTakeHome / 12)`, displayed beside the annual figure and labelled an average.

**6.2 Rounding, and how monthly PF reconciles with the annual CTC and the engine's whole-rupee inputs.**

- Exactly five quantities are rounded, each once: `basic` (annual, `rhu`), `monthlyPF` (monthly, `rhu`, **before** multiplying by 12), `gratuity` (annual, `rhu`), the tax (the engine's own statutory rounding to the nearest ten, section 516, applied inside Tool Pack 17) and the displayed `monthlyAverage` (display only). Every other line is an integer sum, difference or multiple of those, so the lines reconcile exactly.
- **Annual PF is 12 times the rounded monthly contribution**, not `rhu(12% × annual PF base)`. The two can differ by up to ₹6 a year. Pinned example (hand-computed; the oracle must confirm it): basic ₹4,00,050 gives `monthlyBasic` 33,337.50, 12% of it 4,000.50, `monthlyPF` ₹4,001 and `employerPF = employeePF = ₹48,012`; rounding the annual amount instead would give ₹48,006. The monthly route is the one paragraph 18(5) describes; it assumes twelve equal months, and a real payroll with varying months can differ slightly.
- **Engine inputs are integers by construction:** `salary = gross`, `basicPlusDa = basic`, `professionalTax` and `section80C = employeePF + extra80C` are sums of rounded or entered whole rupees. The adapter never rounds, floors or passes a fraction; a non-integer reaching it is a defect and refuses (a guard, not a user error).
- The displayed monthly average is `rhu(annual / 12)`, so twelve times the displayed average can differ from the annual figure by up to ₹6. The annual figure is shown first for that reason.

**6.3 Reconciliation (required).** `CTC = takeHome + employeePF + professionalTax + tax + employerPF + gratuity + otherEmployerCosts`, exactly, in rupees. Because gross is defined by subtraction this holds by construction, so the oracle must compute **every line independently from its rule** and compare, not rely on the identity.

**6.4 Simplified CTC estimate, not a statutory wage determination [DECIDED, D12].** The Scheme's "wages" are the wages defined in section 2(88) of the Code on Social Security (basic, dearness allowance and retaining allowance, plus any amount added back by the one-half rule). P0 uses the user's basic as the PF base and does **not** apply the one-half add-back. The page therefore states, next to the result, that the figures are a **simplified CTC estimate** and **not a determination of statutory wages**, and it makes no claim of compliance with the wage definition. Where the add-back applies (basic below half of total remuneration; for `capped`, only while monthly basic is below the ceiling, since at or above it the PF is already at the ceiling amount), the actual PF can be higher and the take-home shown can be **overstated**; the P1 note and an editable PF amount address this after a legal wording review. The ten per cent classes of S.O. 3582(E) are not detected; the page states "12% of wages" and says a few industries differ. No behaviour that depends on the wage definition is added until the Code on Wages 2(y) text and any notified percentage are verified (0.4 items 2 and 3).

**6.5 Annual liability, TDS, payslip and the monthly average.** The tax is the engine's estimated liability for the whole year on the figures entered. An employer deducts tax at source month by month on its own projection, and the months can differ (bonuses, joining month, changing declarations, the September 2026 ceiling change). Dividing the annual take-home by 12 is useful for comparison but is **not** an exact future payslip and the page never presents it as one. Wording rules (checked by a text test): the labels say "estimated", "annual" or "average"; no label or sentence says "payslip", "credited", "in your account" or "exact" about a figure except in the negative ("not your payslip"). This follows rulebook 8.3.

**6.6 Old-regime comparison [DECIDED, D6].** The comparison is opt-in (section 4). When on, the page needs HRA received, rent paid, the metro choice (when both are above zero) and other 80C investments, each entered explicitly; until then it lists what is still needed and shows no old-regime figure, while the new-regime figure stays current.
- **Included** (and printed beside the figure): the old-regime standard deduction; the professional tax entered; employee PF counted as an 80C investment (the engine applies its own cap); other 80C investments entered; the HRA exemption the engine works out from basic, HRA received, rent paid and the metro choice.
- **Excluded** (printed beside the figure): home-loan interest, health-insurance deductions, the user's own NPS contribution, employer NPS, LTA and other exemptions, other deductions, and other income.
- The label reads, in substance, "Old regime with only the deductions listed. This is not a full old-regime tax assessment." The page gives **no "better regime" verdict**; it links to the Tool Pack 17 comparison for a fuller view. The figure is never described as the user's tax liability or as advice.

**6.7 Refused and unsupported scenarios (no figure is shown for any of them).**
1. CTC missing, not a whole number, zero or negative, or above ₹50,00,000 (refused, never approximated).
2. Basic % outside 1 to 100 or with more than two decimals. A missing PF basis, gratuity choice or basic % is not an error: the result waits with "Still needed".
3. Negative or non-integer professional tax or other employer costs.
4. `gross <= 0` or `gross < basic`; annual take-home below zero.
5. Tool Pack 17 returns `invalid` or `unsupported` for the new-regime call.
6. Old regime only: HRA received plus basic above gross (the engine's rule): a located error on the HRA field, and only the old-regime figure is withheld.
7. Evaluation outside the configuration's validity dates.
- **Order in which the refusals apply (fixed in S3 and pinned by the oracle):** (1) any wrong entry gives `invalid` with an error on each wrong field, even if another entry is missing; (2) otherwise a missing required entry gives `incomplete`; (3) a date outside the validity dates gives `unsupported`; (4) a CTC above the cap gives `unsupported`; (5) an impossible decomposition (gross zero or below, gross below basic) gives `invalid`; (6) professional tax above gross gives `invalid`; (7) a negative take-home gives `invalid`. The old-regime comparison is judged last and on its own (`off`, `incomplete`, `invalid`, `unsupported` or `ok`) and never changes the new-regime result.
- **Not modelled, and stated on the page where it matters:** the 17 September 2026 split month (a run-rate at the current ceiling), ten per cent establishments, state professional-tax rules, EDLI and administrative charges (through "other employer costs"), the one-half add-back, variable pay and bonus, ESI, employer NPS, mid-year joining or leaving, multiple employers, seniors, non-residents and other income.

**6.8 Phase 4 model check.** *Double deduction:* employer PF and gratuity are removed once, when gross is formed, and are never deducted again; employee PF is deducted once from take-home, and its use as an 80C amount is a tax deduction, not a second cash deduction; the pension share sits inside the employer's 12% and is not added again; professional tax is deducted once from take-home and is disallowed for tax by the engine in the new regime. *Circularity:* none. Basic comes from CTC, PF from basic, gross from CTC, tax from gross and the entered amounts, and take-home from those; nothing depends on tax or take-home. *Reconciliation:* exact in rupees (6.3). *Unsupported scenarios:* 6.7. *Conclusion:* the model is fully determined by this section, section 7 and the configuration; no formula is left open.

## 19. Implementation notes

**S3 (2026-10-22): pure module and oracle, implemented locally. No interface, route, catalog entry, registration, shared CSS or existing-tool change; Tool Pack 17's engine and configuration are untouched.**

- **Files added:** `assets/js/calculators/formulas/salary-in-hand.js` (the calculation), `assets/js/calculators/formulas/salary-in-hand-config.js` (the PF rate and ceiling, the gratuity fraction, the CTC cap, validity dates and sources), `tests/fixtures/salary-in-hand-golden.py` (the independent oracle), `tests/fixtures/salary-in-hand-golden.json` (its output, 1003 cases) and `tests/unit/salary-in-hand-golden.test.mjs`. The only specification changes are in 0.2 (items 15 to 17), 0.4 (rows 2 and 9), 6.7, 12, 16, 19 and 20.
- **Input contract:** `calculateSalary(raw, options = {}, config)` with `raw` = `{ ctc, basicPercent, pfBasis, gratuityIncluded, otherEmployerCosts?, professionalTax?, oldRegime?: { enabled, hraReceived, rentPaid, highCity?, other80C } }`. Amounts are whole-rupee integers (numbers). `basicPercent` is a number or a digit string, 1 to 100, at most two decimals. `pfBasis` is `capped`, `full-basic` or `none`. `gratuityIncluded` is a boolean. `options.asOf` is an optional `YYYY-MM-DD` string supplied by the caller; the module never reads a clock.
- **Output contract:** a deeply frozen object. `status` is `ok`, `incomplete` (`missing` lists the absent required entries), `invalid` (`errors` keyed by field; `decomposition` for an impossible split) or `unsupported` (`reason` is `ctc-above-cap`, `outside-validity` or `tax-engine-refused`). On `ok`: `ctc`, `basic`, `pfBasis`, `monthlyPf`, `employerPf`, `employeePf`, `gratuity`, `otherEmployerCosts`, `gross`, `professionalTax`, `new` and `old` (each `{ tax, annualTakeHome, monthlyAverage }`; `old` also has `status`, `included` and `excluded` ids, or is `off`, `incomplete`, `invalid` or `unsupported`), and a `reconciliation` block. Nothing is formatted.
- **Conventions as built:** integer arithmetic only; `roundHalfUp(n, d)` is `floor((2n + d) / 2d)` on integers; the full-basic contribution is `rhu(12 × basic / 1200)` (the monthly basic is never rounded) and the capped contribution is `rhu(12 × 25,000 / 100)` = 3,000 from a monthly basic of 25,000 upward; annual PF is 12 × the monthly amount; gratuity is `rhu(basic × 15 / 312)`. The two engine calls of section 7 are made as specified (new regime always; old regime only when complete and valid).
- **Independence of the oracle:** the Python reference follows the specification's own steps in `Fraction` arithmetic (a monthly basic of basic / 12, then 12% of it, then round half up), re-derives every rounding with `Decimal`, asserts the identity and the pinned examples before writing, takes the tax from Tool Pack 17's reference loaded as a library, and neither reads nor imports the JavaScript. The JavaScript module uses the simplified integer algebra; agreement on 1003 cases is therefore a real cross-check.
- **Verification run (local, focused):** `node --test tests/unit/salary-in-hand-golden.test.mjs`, 34 tests in 6 groups, all passing; the oracle's 1003 cases (835 ok, 154 invalid, 10 incomplete, 4 unsupported; the old-regime comparison off, ok, invalid and incomplete) match with no mismatch. Mutation spot-checks on the rounding, the monthly-versus-annual PF rounding, the old-regime 80C amount and a ceiling-boundary change were caught by the tests, except a ceiling comparison shifted by up to 12 rupees of annual basic, an equivalent change because the rounded contribution is 3,000 either way in that range.
- **Deviations and findings:** (1) the invariant "monotonicity in CTC" in the first draft of section 12 was wrong and is corrected there (the rebate-edge exception); the engine and the independent reference agree, so the formula is unchanged. (2) The specification did not fix the order in which refusals apply; it is now fixed in 6.7. (3) Results are deeply frozen and the validity check takes a caller-supplied date, to keep the module clock-free. (4) Not done in S3, by design: any interface, wording, formatting, registration, browser test or release step.
- **Not verified:** the tax engine's own 238 cases were not rerun (unchanged); no browser, build or full regression was run; the independent financial or payroll review has not happened.
